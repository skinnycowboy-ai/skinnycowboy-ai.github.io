# Build it. Test it. Write down what happened.

These working technical notes are developed by Mark Ivankovich for his Red Hat
OpenShift Platform & AI Specialist role across state, local government, and
education.

The library names Red Hat OpenShift, Red Hat AI, OpenShift Virtualization,
Ansible Automation Platform, and related products directly when they are part of
the technical subject. The SLED branch reorganizes the same material around
public-sector contexts and constraints.

## Editorial boundaries

- This is role-based field enablement, not a personal commercial offering.
- These notes are not official Red Hat product documentation or support guidance.
- Current product claims must be verified against official Red Hat documentation
  before public publication or customer reuse.
- Project Vaquero lab evidence is not production sizing, resilience, security,
  supportability, or disaster-recovery assurance.
- No Salesforce integration, customer records, contact details, opportunity
  data, or lead-capture forms.
- No automatic social publishing. LinkedIn, YouTube, Facebook, and X derivatives
  remain human-reviewed drafts for approved systems.

## Working model

1. Research a technical problem and the relevant Red Hat capabilities.
2. Write a product-specific architecture note.
3. Test the parts that fit Project Vaquero.
4. Record evidence, failure conditions, and limits.
5. Review the article before public publication or outreach reuse.
