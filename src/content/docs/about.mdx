# Practical technical guidance for SLED teams

This resource center is authored by Mark Ivankovich for his Red Hat OpenShift
Platform & AI Specialist role across state, local government, and education.
It is designed for technology leaders, architects, platform teams, application
teams, operations teams, and security teams evaluating modernization choices.

## What you will find

- Public-sector technology paths organized around outcomes and constraints
- Product-specific guides for Red Hat OpenShift, Red Hat OpenShift AI,
  OpenShift Virtualization, and Red Hat Ansible Automation Platform
- Long-form field notes that make architecture assumptions and tradeoffs visible
- Project Vaquero lab records with observable results and explicit limits
- Lightwell from IBM and Red Hat as a commercial open source remediation offering

## How to use the material

Use a page to prepare better architecture questions, identify dependencies,
define a bounded validation exercise, or understand what evidence a technical
decision should preserve. Then verify the proposed design against current Red
Hat documentation and review it with the appropriate Red Hat and customer
technical teams.

## Important scope

- This is independently authored role-based technical content. It is not
  official Red Hat product documentation, support guidance, or an approved
  production architecture.
- Product capabilities, versions, subscriptions, lifecycle, and support
  boundaries must be checked against current official Red Hat sources.
- A Project Vaquero result describes a recorded lab workflow. It is not
  production sizing, availability, resilience, security approval,
  supportability, or disaster-recovery assurance.
- Lightwell is a joint IBM and Red Hat initiative with commercial subscription
  offerings. Availability, eligibility, and package coverage must be verified
  against current official Lightwell sources.
- This public site contains no customer records, contact information, account
  plans, pricing, or opportunity data.
