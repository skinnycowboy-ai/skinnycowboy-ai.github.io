# Connect a customer signal to a technical proof

This hub turns the technical library into a small set of reusable SLED buying
motions. Start with an observed problem, ask enough questions to expose the real
constraint, and choose the smallest proof that can support a next decision.

The product libraries remain the canonical source for architecture notes and
claims. This page only connects those notes into field-ready paths.

:::caution[Field-use boundary]
This is role-based field enablement, not official Red Hat campaign or product
documentation. Do not place customer names, contacts, account details,
opportunity data, pricing, or CRM exports here. Recheck product capabilities,
subscriptions, lifecycle, and support boundaries in current official Red Hat
documentation before customer use.
:::

## Use the hub in six steps

1. **Name the signal.** Record the public-sector problem without adding account
   or personally identifiable information.
2. **Choose a motion.** Select the closest path below; do not force a product
   answer before discovery.
3. **Test the assumptions.** Use the discovery questions to find ownership,
   dependencies, constraints, and risk.
4. **Select the smallest proof.** Prefer one representative workflow with a
   clear start, finish, failure condition, and rollback.
5. **Agree on evidence.** Define what someone must be able to inspect before the
   proof starts.
6. **Make the next decision explicit.** A useful proof earns a decision, not
   merely a demonstration.

## Motion selector

| Buying motion | Typical signal | First technical proof | Evidence to retain | Decision it should enable |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Virtualization modernization | VM lifecycle, cost, capacity, or skills pressure | Migrate one representative VM with documented mappings and rollback | Inventory, plan, mappings, logs, validation, and rollback result | Whether to proceed to a workload wave assessment |
| Application platform standardization | Fragmented delivery, runtime, or policy controls | Promote one application through a declarative Git workflow | Repository change, sync result, policy result, and rollback | Whether a shared platform pattern is viable |
| Disconnected and controlled operations | Restricted networks or controlled content movement | Mirror a bounded content set and rehearse its transfer | Image-set configuration, manifests, logs, checksums, and exceptions | Whether the content path is operable and governable |
| Private and governed AI | Sensitive data, model access, or resource-governance concerns | Serve one approved model behind an explicit access boundary | Model provenance, access policy, endpoint test, monitoring, and evaluation result | Whether to expand the AI workload evaluation |
| Project Lightwell closed-loop remediation | Findings arrive faster than teams can safely remediate them | Move one finding through proposal, test, human approval, deploy, and verification | Finding, change, test, approval, deployment, verification, and rollback evidence | Whether the closed-loop pattern deserves a broader pilot |

## 1. Virtualization modernization

**Signal.** Existing virtualization decisions are being driven by platform
lifecycle, cost, datacenter change, capacity, or operational skills. Some
workloads may remain virtual machines, while others may be candidates for
modernization.

**Discovery questions.** Which workloads must move, stay, retire, or change?
What are the storage, network, backup, disaster-recovery, identity, licensing,
and maintenance-window dependencies? Who accepts the migration result, and what
would trigger rollback?

**Capabilities to evaluate.** OpenShift Virtualization and Migration Toolkit for
Virtualization, with the surrounding OpenShift networking, storage, security,
and operations design.

**Smallest useful proof.** Inventory one representative workload, document its
mappings and dependencies, execute or rehearse a migration plan, validate the
application outcome, and prove the rollback path.

## 2. Application platform standardization

**Signal.** Teams use inconsistent delivery paths, runtime controls, promotion
methods, or policy checks. Releases are slowed by handoffs and the evidence for
what changed is difficult to reconstruct.

**Discovery questions.** Where does application ownership change hands? Which
configuration is declarative today? How are changes promoted, approved,
observed, and rolled back? Which platform controls must be consistent, and which
choices must remain with application teams?

**Capabilities to evaluate.** Red Hat OpenShift and Red Hat OpenShift GitOps,
plus the organization-specific source, build, registry, policy, and observability
systems already in use.

**Smallest useful proof.** Put one representative application and its
environment configuration through a Git-based promotion flow. Retain the change,
sync, policy, runtime, and rollback evidence.

## 3. Disconnected and controlled operations

**Signal.** A restricted network, sovereignty requirement, approval process, or
security boundary controls how software content enters an environment. The
important problem is the full content lifecycle, not only installation.

**Discovery questions.** What content is allowed to cross the boundary? Who
approves it? Where is it staged and scanned? How are integrity, provenance,
exceptions, update cadence, capacity, and rollback handled? What happens when a
required dependency is missing?

**Capabilities to evaluate.** OpenShift disconnected installation and update
workflows, the oc-mirror plugin v2, registry architecture, and controlled
automation for repeatable transfer and evidence capture.

**Smallest useful proof.** Define a bounded image set, mirror it to disk, move it
through the approved boundary, publish it to the destination registry, and
rehearse consumption or update. Capture manifests, logs, checksums, exceptions,
and rollback steps.

## 4. Private and governed AI

**Signal.** An AI use case involves sensitive data, controlled model access,
resource governance, provenance, or a disconnected operating environment. The
organization needs a governed workload path rather than an isolated model demo.

**Discovery questions.** Where may data and models reside? Who can access which
model? How are quotas, accelerators, model provenance, evaluation, monitoring,
and audit evidence handled? Who owns the model-serving service after the proof?

**Capabilities to evaluate.** Red Hat OpenShift AI Self-Managed and the relevant
OpenShift platform foundation for model serving, access, resource management,
monitoring, and disconnected operations.

**Smallest useful proof.** Serve one approved model behind an explicit access
boundary. Exercise one representative request and retain the model provenance,
endpoint, access, resource, monitoring, and evaluation evidence.

## 5. Project Lightwell closed-loop remediation

:::caution[Project Lightwell boundary]
Project Lightwell is a **field-developed buying motion** and demonstration
concept. It is **not a Red Hat product**, SKU, supported reference architecture,
or promise of autonomous remediation.
:::

**Signal.** Security or operational findings arrive faster than application and
platform teams can safely remediate them. The organization needs a visible,
controlled path from finding to verified change.

**Discovery questions.** Which findings are eligible? Who owns the affected
application? Where must human approval remain? What testing and policy gates are
required? Who can deploy, what triggers rollback, and how long must the evidence
be retained?

**Capabilities to evaluate.** Depending on the approved scenario, the motion
may evaluate Red Hat OpenShift, Red Hat OpenShift AI, Red Hat Ansible Automation
Platform, Red Hat Advanced Cluster Security for Kubernetes, and Git-based
workflows. This is a composable field pattern, not a new product bundle.

**Smallest useful proof.** Take one non-production finding through proposal,
test, policy checks, explicit human approval, deployment, verification, and an
evidence bundle. Stop safely when a gate fails and demonstrate rollback.

## Project Vaquero proof boundary

[Project Vaquero](/project-vaquero/) can test bounded workflows, integrations,
failure conditions, and evidence collection. A successful lab proof does not
establish production sizing, availability, resilience, security, compliance,
supportability, or disaster recovery. Record both what the proof demonstrates
and what remains untested.

## Human-reviewed outreach handoff

Once a motion has a reviewed technical note and evidence boundary, it can seed
channel-specific drafts:

- a short LinkedIn observation with discovery questions and a link to the note;
- a YouTube outline that walks through the signal, architecture, proof, and
  limitations;
- a Facebook excerpt suitable for an approved public-sector community; or
- an X post or thread that points readers to the durable technical source.

These remain drafts. This repository does not publish automatically to external
networks, store outreach contacts, or track account activity. Use only approved
Red Hat systems and human review for actual outreach.
