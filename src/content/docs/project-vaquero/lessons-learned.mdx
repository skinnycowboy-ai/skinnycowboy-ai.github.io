# Project Vaquero lessons learned

Power, noise, hardware, network access, and rebuild time force every component
to justify its place. That discipline scales even when the lab itself does not.

## Rules worth carrying forward

1. Separate proposed, accepted, installed, and validated state.
2. Treat missing evidence as unknown, not healthy.
3. Record failure conditions before changing the design.
4. Explain exactly what a demonstration proves and what remains outside scope.
5. Prefer rebuildable automation over undocumented manual recovery.
6. Validate rollback and cleanup ownership before accepting a workflow.
7. Keep customer data and opportunity records outside the lab and this site.

## Constraints are useful architecture inputs

The lab is adjacent to a working office. Acoustic limits matter. The platform
must fit within known electrical, cooling, rack, network, and budget constraints.
These limits make resource tradeoffs visible instead of allowing the design to
assume unlimited infrastructure.

## A demonstration needs an evidence contract

Before a demonstration is considered ready, document:

- The question it answers
- The exact architecture and software state
- The preconditions and test procedure
- The expected and observed results
- The cleanup or rollback path
- The person who owns each operational boundary
- The claims that remain unsupported

## Production claims stay outside the lab

Project Vaquero supports technical learning and field demonstrations. Production
architecture, sizing, resilience, security, support, and disaster-recovery
decisions require the normal Red Hat and customer validation processes.
